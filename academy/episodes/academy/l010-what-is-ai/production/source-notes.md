SOURCE CHECK: "What AI is and is not" (fetched 2026-10-07)

Quote status: [V] = I read it in the raw page/PDF text. [T] = WebFetch extraction only (direct download blocked), not independently checked.

Two findings that change the lesson design:
- **"Rules" is not cleanly "not AI".** UNICEF and OECD count rule-following systems as AI; Oak National Academy says fixed-step devices are not. Present the rules / not-AI line as fuzzy.
- **Day of AI has no Grade 3–6 unit on its curriculum page.** The listing shows Pre-K, K, G1, G2, G7, G8 plus topical units for ages 11–18. Slides and guides sit behind a free login. The rule-vs-data comparison is in Grade 7.

**1a. Rule-based: supported**
- Oak National Academy (England), KS2 Year 4 lesson "What is artificial intelligence?", https://www.thenational.academy/pupils/lessons/what-is-artificial-intelligence/video, no visible date. [V] "A computer can follow fixed rules. For example, a calculator always adds numbers using the same steps."
- OECD, Explanatory memorandum on the updated OECD definition of an AI system, Feb 2024 (university-hosted copy: https://comite-etica.upc.edu/ca/actualitat/media/8-sti-explanatory-memorandum-on-the-updated-oecd-definition-of-an-ai-system_final_v3.pdf/@@download/file/8%20-%20STI%20-%20Explanatory%20memorandum%20on%20the%20updated%20OECD%20definition%20of%20an%20AI%20system_FINAL_V3.pdf). [V] "rules dictate the action to be taken by the AI system according to the current circumstance. For example, a driving system might have a rule, “If the traffic light is red, stop.”"
- Limit: OECD is describing a kind of AI system here, not non-AI.

**1b. Machine learning: supported**
- Same OECD memo. [V] "Machine learning is a set of techniques that allows machines to improve their performance and usually generate models in an automated manner through exposure to training data, which can help identify patterns and regularities rather than through explicit instructions from a human."
- IBM, "The 2026 Guide to Machine Learning", published 22 September 2025, https://www.ibm.com/think/machine-learning. [V] "…make decisions or predictions without explicit, hard-coded instructions."
- Code.org lesson plan, https://lesson-plans.code.org/csd7-2026/20260324130350/teacher-lesson-plans/Lesson-1-Introduction-to-Machine-Learning.pdf. [V] "Machine Learning: How computers recognize patterns and make decisions without being explicitly programmed"

**1c. Generative AI: supported**
- UNICEF Guidance on AI and Children 3.0, December 2025, https://www.unicef.org/innocenti/media/11991/file/UNICEF-Innocenti-Guidance-on-AI-and-Children-3-2025.pdf. [V] "instead of only recognizing and processing data, they can also generate new content by using statistical patterns to predict what comes next in language, images and other media."
- Microsoft, "How Does Generative AI Work", https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/ai/ai-101/how-does-generative-ai-work, no date. [V] "Generative AI uses advanced machine learning techniques to analyze large datasets and generate new content based on the context, style, structure, and tone of the original data."

**2. Confident errors, so check: supported**
- OpenAI Help, "Does ChatGPT tell the truth?", https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8313428-does-chatgpt-tell-the-truth, "updated 2 months ago". [T] "But like any language model, it can produce incorrect or misleading outputs. Sometimes, it might sound confident—even when it's wrong." and "verify important information from reliable sources."
- Google, "Guide your child's Gemini Apps experience", https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/16109150?hl=en, no date. [V] "Gemini is new and evolving, and can hallucinate and present inaccurate information as factual."
- Anthropic Consumer Terms, effective October 8, 2025, https://www.anthropic.com/legal/consumer-terms. [V] "Outputs may not always be accurate and may contain material inaccuracies even if they appear accurate because of their level of detail or specificity." and "You should not rely on any Outputs or Actions without independently confirming their accuracy."
- UNESCO generative-AI guidance: full text not fetched (403 / robots).

**3. Bias from data: supported**
- 教育部「中小學使用『生成式人工智慧』注意事項2.1」（學生版；115年2月11日核定）, school-hosted copy: https://jr.hs.ntnu.edu.tw/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/中小學使用「生成式人工智慧」注意事項2.1.pdf. [V]「『生成式人工智慧』工具的資料來源是歷史紀錄，如果這些資料本身具有成見或錯誤，那麼使用『生成式人工智慧』工具的結果也會存在偏差或錯誤，因為這些工具無法自行判斷結果的正確性和合理性。」
- NIST AI RMF 1.0, January 2023, https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.100-1.pdf. [V] "AI systems can potentially increase the speed and scale of biases and perpetuate and amplify harms"
- UNICEF AI guide for parents, November 2021, https://www.unicef.org/innocenti/media/1361/file/UNICEF-Global-Insight-AI%20guide%20for%20parents-2021.pdf. [V] "Bias occurs when an AI system produces results that are systemically prejudiced, due to assumptions made during the development process, prejudices in the training data or design errors."

**4. "Understanding" and "feelings": partly supported (sourced wording exists; it is not a scientific consensus statement)**
- Google, same page as claim 2. [V] "Help your child understand that although Gemini is conversational, it's not a person and cannot think for itself or feel emotions."
- UNICEF 3.0. [V] "teach children to develop accurate mental models of AI systems as data-driven applications rather than sentient machines" and "companion apps are not real or capable of human emotion".
- Oak. [V] "An AI system generates answers, but it does not understand them. It does not think or feel like a human."
- MIT Sloan Teaching & Learning Technologies (cited by 教育部), https://mitsloanedtech.mit.edu/ai/basics/addressing-ai-hallucinations-and-bias/. [V] "Generative AI models function like advanced autocomplete tools: They’re designed to predict the next word or sequence based on observed patterns."
- Day of AI Kindergarten unit, https://dayofai.org/units/kindergarten-machines-all-around-us. [V] machines "do not think or experience the world like humans".
- Limit: attribute these ("Google 提醒家長…"); none is a research finding.

**5. Everyday examples: partly supported**
- Spam: Google Workspace Blog, June 1, 2017, https://workspace.google.com/blog/product-announcements/keeping-your-company-data-safe-new-security-updates-gmail. [V] "Machine learning helps Gmail block sneaky spam and phishing messages from showing up in your inbox with over 99.9 percent accuracy." The figure is from 2017.
- Face ID: Apple, "About Face ID advanced technology", published September 18, 2026, https://support.apple.com/en-us/102381. [V] "Face ID uses the TrueDepth camera and machine learning for a secure authentication solution." The same page says the false-match probability is higher "among children under the age of 13".
- Photos: Apple, "Photos & Privacy", 2025-12-12, https://apple.com/legal/privacy/data/en/photos/. [V] "Photos uses on-device machine learning to deliver a personalized experience, organize photos and videos… This on-device analysis includes scene classification, people and pets identification". It does not literally say "search by content".
- Recommendations: UNICEF 3.0. [V] "Most AI applications in use today – from recommendation engines to smart robots – rely on machine learning techniques that recognize patterns in data."
- Voice: Apple ML Research, October 1, 2017, https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/hey-siri. [V] "The “Hey Siri” detector uses a Deep Neural Network (DNN)".
- Explicit contrast: Oak. [V] "So, a voice assistant, predictive text, and face-unlock use AI. Calculator, traffic lights, and a paper dictionary do not." and "If it only follows simple fixed steps, it is not AI."
- Not found: any source classifying a thermostat, timer or motion-sensor light.

**7. Privacy: supported**

**8. Taiwan guidance: supported**
- 教育部 2.1 (above). [V]「中小學學生使用『生成式人工智慧』工具應遵守各平臺註冊年齡限制及相關規範。」「…在校應於教師引導或指導下使用，若為非在校使用，亦請家長陪伴使用。」
- 行政院, 112-08-31, https://www.ey.gov.tw/Page/448DE008087A1971/40c1a925-121d-4b6b-8f40-7e9e1a5401f2. The page carries only a summary; the ten points were seen only in a news reprint (https://www.1111.com.tw/news/jobns/152993). [V, secondary]「各機關不可完全信任生成式AI產出之資訊」.

**9. No single definition: supported**
- OECD.AI, November 29, 2023, https://oecd.ai/en/wonk/ai-system-definition-update. [V] "Obtaining consensus on a definition for an AI system in any sector or group of experts has proven to be a complicated task."
- OECD definition (memo, [V]): "An AI system is a machine-based system that, for explicit or implicit objectives, infers, from the input it receives, how to generate outputs such as predictions, content, recommendations, or decisions that can influence physical or virtual environments."
- 人工智慧基本法 第三條（總統府公報第7837號，115年1月14日）, https://www.president.gov.tw/File/Doc/80165b6d-cb49-4b49-952f-56e1e6abe51b. [V]「本法所稱人工智慧，指具自主運行能力之系統，該系統透過輸入或感測，經由機器學習及演算法，可為明確或隱含之目標實現預測、內容、建議或決策等影響實體或虛擬環境之產出。」
- UNICEF 3.0. [V] "Simply put, AI systems work by either following explicit rules, learning from examples (through supervised or unsupervised learning) or improving through trial and error (reinforcement learning)."
- NIST's definition differs again ("engineered or machine-based system…", adapted from OECD 2019 and ISO/IEC 22989:2022). No official ISO summary was fetched.
- UNESCO AI competency framework for students (2024), https://unesdoc.unesco.org/in/rest/annotationSVC/DownloadWatermarkedAttachment/attach_import_be395de4-719f-49fa-94f9-b0381426f8c5. [T] "facilitate students to understand what AI is and is not".

**Do NOT say**
- "Rule-based programs are not AI" as settled fact. Say "simple fixed steps", and that experts draw the line differently.
- "A thermostat, timer or motion-sensor light is not AI" as sourced; only calculator, traffic lights and paper dictionary are (Oak).
- That AI understands, thinks or has feelings; or, unattributed, that science has proved it understands nothing.
- That AI knows when it is wrong or checks its own facts.
- That children can open their own ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude accounts. Claude is 18+.
- "UNESCO bans AI under 13". The UNESCO press release (7 September 2023, https://gcedclearinghouse.org/en/node/129359) reads [T] "sets an age limit of 13 for the use of AI tools in the classroom"; the guidance itself was not verified.
- "Gmail blocks 99.9% of spam" as a current figure.
- "Face ID is generative AI", or that it is infallible.
- "Everything you type is used for training."
- Anything from the OpenAI "What is ChatGPT?" FAQ. The WebFetch output looked outdated ("fine-tuned from GPT-3.5").
